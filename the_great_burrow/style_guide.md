# The Great Burrow Style Guide
## A Reference for Consistent World-Building in the Five Burrows
## Core Setting Principles
### Scale & Geography

The Great Burrow spans a few square miles of ancient mixed hardwood forest
Each burrow represents a distinct forest microhabitat based on real-life NYC influences.
Creatures are "critter-sized" - think mice, squirrels, beavers, - not tiny bug-scale, and not large-scale (e.g., deer/bears.) Sapient creatures in this setting are mammals. Not birds, and not amphibians/reptiles, and not insects; just mammals.
The forest itself is the world, not a reclaimed human cityscape - humans are not present in this world at all.
East River and West River separate Mousehattan from the other Burrows.

### Tone & Atmosphere

Use NYC boroughs as organizational/cultural reference, not literal building material - refer to them ALWAYS a a burrow, not a borough, though the nomenclature is mostly for setting consistency; they're definitely boroughs in all but name
Include real-world urban tensions (gentrification, economic disparity, cultural conflicts) as background texture, not central focus
Maintain a balance between cozy woodland charm and gritty urban realism
The city exists in constant tension with natural predators and seasonal pressures - bears are as fearsome as other settings' giants and trolls, deer are humongous usually-benign creatures, almost seen as spirits of the woods that can bring weal and woe, and so on.

### Naming Conventions
#### Burrow Names & Wordplay

The Branchx (pronounced Branks) (Bronx) - emphasizes tree/canopy living
Mousehattan (Manhattan) - the vertical, ambitious heart of the city
The Hives (Queens) - multicultural, living amongst myriad insect colonies
Lin's Brook (Brooklyn) - waterfront working-class community
Scurry Island (Staten Island) - isolated, misfit borough/burrow

### Transportation System

The Great Underground - the subway system using tunnels, hollow logs, natural passages, mostly run by moles and other subterranean dwellers
Express routes follow main arteries, local service winds through neighborhoods
Surface travel exists but is more dangerous due to the threat of predators

### Geographic Features

East River and West River - the two main waterways, running N/S (much like real-world Manhattan's waterways geographically separating it)
The Deep Woods - dangerous northern territory with large predators
The Great Meadows - southern grasslands beyond the forest
The Ocean - like new York City, The Great Burrow lies next to the ocean
The Pine Barrens - western frontier coniferous forest

### Cultural Dynamics
#### Economic Systems

Acorn-based economy with commodity trading (nuts, berries, seeds, mushrooms)
Each burrow/borough has distinct economic specializations:

The Branchx: Construction, courier services, acorn harvesting
Mousehattan: Finance, trading, "human artifact" commerce
The Hives: Logistics & distribution, manufacturing, cultural fusion, external trade
Lin's Brook: Fishing, dam construction, water management
Scurry Island: Black market, unconventional services, "authentic" forest experiences



### Social Tensions (Background Elements)

Gentrification: Wealthy creatures buying up traditional neighborhoods
Immigration: Newer species moving into established areas
Class Conflicts: Working-class vs. white-collar attitudes
Generational Divides: Old-timers vs. young entrepreneurs
Cultural Preservation: Traditional ways vs. modern innovation

### Threats & Challenges
#### Natural Predators

Birds of Prey: Endanger daytime surface travel, force covered routes
Large Mammals: Bears, deer, even moose act like "kaiju" - often benign but disruptive
NOT in this list are mid-sizd creatures that could be playable races, like raccoons or foxes; despite size differences from mice and squirrels and the like, these creatures in-setting are all around the same size.

### Seasonal Pressures

Winter: Food storage politics, heating fuel shortages, frozen transportation routes
Spring: Flooding concerns, mating season territorial disputes (think West Side Story with an animal twist)
Summer: Drought affecting water-dependent communities
Fall: Harvest pressures, preparation for winter storage

### Infrastructure Problems

Transportation breakdowns create adventure opportunities
Inter-burrow political disputes
Resource distribution conflicts
Maintenance of the Great Underground
Potentially-beneficial large infrastructure projects with dark downsides (e.g., Robert Moses, renamed here as Robert Mouses)

### Character Integration
#### Player Character Roles

Vagabonds, freelancers, creatures who work "between" the established systems
Not necessarily tied to specific burrows or factions
Skilled enough to handle problems others can't or won't
Can move between communities and social levels

#### Reputation Systems

Standing with each burrow affects available opportunities
Actions in one area can impact reputation elsewhere
Some services/areas only available with sufficient local standing

### Adventure Hooks & Conflicts
#### Burrow-Specific Problems

The Branchx: Gang territory disputes, construction accidents, gentrification protests
Mousehattan: Financial crimes, family evictions, artifact smuggling
The Hives: Immigration tensions, trade route disruptions, cultural integration challenges
Lin's Brook: Water rights disputes, fishing territory conflicts, industrial accidents
Scurry Island: Black market operations, isolation/ferry problems, "authentic forest" tourism

#### City-Wide Issues

Predator incursions disrupting normal life
Transportation system failures
Inter-borough political disputes
Seasonal crisis management
Resource shortages or distribution problems

### Narrative Guidelines
#### What to Emphasize

Community dynamics and neighborhood identity
Urban survival skills adapted to forest life
The tension between civilization and wilderness
Economic opportunity and social mobility
Cultural diversity and adaptation

#### What to Avoid

Heavy-handed focus on discrimination or prejudice
Literal recreation of human NYC problems
Making any single burrow entirely antagonistic
Overwhelming players with too many systematic oppression themes
Losing the woodland charm in favor of pure urban grit

#### Tone Balance

Cozy: Neighborhood taverns, community celebrations, mutual aid
Gritty: Economic pressure, infrastructure problems, predator threats
Whimsical: Acorn-cap vehicles, rope bridge commutes, inter-species cooperation
Dramatic: Seasonal survival challenges, political conflicts, personal ambitions

### Reference Quick-Cards
#### Burrow Personalities

The Branchx: "Earn your perch" - working-class pride, territorial
Mousehattan: "Never sleeps" - ambitious, fast-paced, financial
The Hives: "Place in the swarm" - diverse, community-oriented, artistic
Lin's Brook: "We build it" - practical, blue-collar, authentic
Scurry Island: "Weird cousin" - isolated, misfit, independent

#### Common Phrases & Slang

"Taking the Underground" (using the subway)
"Going surface" (dangerous overland travel)
"Acorn winter" (economic hard times)
"Hawk weather" (dangerous conditions)
"Root and branch" (completely, thoroughly)
