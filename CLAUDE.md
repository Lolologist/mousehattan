# The Great Burrow - Claude Instructions

## Project Overview
This is **The Great Burrow**, a tabletop RPG setting inspired by NYC boroughs but populated by woodland creatures in an ancient forest. Think "Zootopia meets NYC" but with mice, squirrels, beavers, and other small mammals building an impossible urban civilization while constantly threatened by giants (bears, deer) and predators (hawks, owls).

## Core Setting Principles
- **Scale**: Creatures are "critter-sized" (mice, squirrels, beavers) - not tiny bug-scale, not large-scale
- **Sapient Species**: ONLY mammals - no birds, amphibians, reptiles, or insects as player characters
- **Tone**: Balance "cozy woodland charm and gritty urban realism" 
- **Geography**: Five burrows connected by underground subway, separated by East/West Rivers
- **NYC Inspiration**: Use NYC boroughs as organizational/cultural reference, always call them "burrows" not "boroughs"

## Writing Style Guidelines

### Tone Balance
- Maintain balance between whimsical woodland charm and realistic urban challenges
- Include real urban tensions (gentrification, economic disparity, cultural conflicts) as background texture
- Never lose the woodland charm in favor of pure urban grit
- Avoid heavy-handed focus on discrimination - keep it about urban survival and opportunity

### Species Consistency
- Each species has distinct cultural traits and vocal patterns (see species.md)
- Mice: Precise, formal speech; old money families
- Rats: Loud, confident entrepreneurs; "make holes bigger"
- Squirrels: Height-obsessed; branch level = social status
- Beavers: Judge others by work quality; construction metaphors
- And many more - always check species.md for details

### NYC Cultural Influences
- Working class patterns: Chipmunks, squirrels, beavers, otters
- Business/professional: Mice, rats, some rabbits  
- Immigrant communities: Guinea pigs with code-switching speech
- Neighborhood variations: Each burrow has distinct local speech patterns

## Common Commands & Workflows

### File Reading Priority
When exploring the setting:
1. Start with `The_Great_Burrow.md` for overview
2. Check `style_guide.md` for consistency rules
3. Reference `species.md` for character details
4. Dive into specific `Burrows/` files as needed
5. If working on Genesys-specific material, reference the system_specific_rules/genesys folder.

### Consistency Checks
- Always verify species details against `species.md`
- Check tone balance against `style_guide.md` 
- Ensure burrow descriptions match established geography, tone, and materials
- Maintain the "cozy woodland charm + gritty urban realism" balance

### When Making Changes
- Follow the style guide principles religiously
- Maintain consistency with existing content
- Ask for clarification if unsure about tone balance
- Consider how changes affect the overall setting coherence

## Species Quick Reference
- **Mice**: Old money, precise speech, formal manners
- **Rats**: New money, loud entrepreneurs, "make holes bigger"  
- **Squirrels**: Height hierarchy, winter storage obsessed
- **Chipmunks**: Speed culture, courier crews, rapid-fire speech
- **Beavers**: Master builders, judge by work quality
- **Otters**: Waterfront workers, competitive, water metaphors
- **Rabbits**: Agricultural aristocracy, honey wealth
- **Guinea Pigs**: Recent immigrants, musical speech, terracing techniques
- **Opossums**: Survivalists, "play dead" to society
- **Raccoons**: Clever operators, literally "wash" stolen goods

Remember: The forest itself IS the world - no humans exist in this setting. Every creature dreams of making it in the big city while trying not to get eaten, stepped on, or priced out.

Note for writing information down - don't use tables, GM Binder is currently bugged in its implementation.
